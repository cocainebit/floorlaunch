---
title: "Insurance Fund"
description: "The per-market SOL pool that is the bounded counterparty to cash-settled shorts."
---

Each market carries its own insurance fund. It is not a shared protocol pool: a blowup in one market cannot drain the backstop of another. On the Meteora path the fund plays a bigger role than in v1: it is the **bounded counterparty** to the market's [cash-settled shorts](/concepts/shorts). It pays short profits, absorbs short losses, and tops up liquidation shortfalls.

## How it is funded

In v1 the fund was seeded from a share of the internal curve's raise at graduation. There is no such raise on the Meteora path, so the fund is seeded **permissionlessly** with `fund_insurance`:

```
fund_insurance(amount)   // transfers `amount` lamports into the market's insurance fund
```

Anyone can call it: the creator seeding their own market, a hedger who wants short capacity to exist, or the protocol. The lamports live in the market's program-controlled SOL vault, tracked in `Market.insurance_lamports`, so insurance can never be spent as anything else.

Because the fund is the counterparty to shorts, a market's practical short capacity tracks its balance alongside the `max_open_interest` cap: a market with a small fund can back only small short open interest.

## What it pays for

**Short profits.** When a short closes in profit (the index fell below its entry), the profit is paid to the owner from the insurance fund. See [`close_position`](/concepts/shorts#closing-close-position).

**Short losses (inflow).** When a short closes at a loss, the loss is deducted from its collateral and paid **into** the insurance fund. So a market where shorts are, on net, wrong (the collectible rises) accumulates insurance; a market where shorts are right pays out of it.

**Liquidation shortfalls.** When a position is so far underwater that its collateral cannot cover the liquidator's target payout, the fund tops up the difference:

```
shortfall = target_payout - collateral
topup     = min(shortfall, insurance_lamports)
```

This keeps liquidation profitable even for badly late liquidations, which keeps liquidators showing up. See [Liquidations](/concepts/liquidations).

## When it runs dry

The fund is a buffer, not a guarantee:

- Short profits and liquidator payouts are capped at the available insurance. If the fund is exhausted, a closing short may not be paid its full profit, and a liquidator may receive less than the target payout, until the fund is replenished with `fund_insurance`.
- Nothing else breaks: the market keeps trading on Meteora, existing positions keep accruing funding, and the fund becomes relevant again once it is topped up.

<Warning>
A depleted insurance fund is the signal that a market's short side is winning faster than the fund can pay, or that its parameters were too aggressive for its underlying (collateral ratios too low, open-interest cap too large, breaker too loose). Listing policy and adequate seeding exist to prevent reaching this state; see [Listing a Market](/guides/listing-a-market).
</Warning>

## Reading it

`Market.insurance_lamports` is the live balance. `fund_insurance` deposits, closing shorts, and liquidations all move it; each `Liquidated` and short-close event lets you reconstruct drawdowns from payout versus collateral.
