Shorts post SOL collateral against a notional valued at the [price index](/concepts/price-index). If the index rises far enough against a position, its collateral no longer safely covers the loss and anyone may liquidate it. Liquidation is **cash-only**: no tokens change hands.

## The collateral ratio

```
notional_value = size * index_twap / 1_000_000_000_000
CR             = collateral / notional_value
```

`size` is the position's short notional and funding is folded into its settleable PnL (see [Shorts](/concepts/shorts)). A position becomes liquidatable when:

```
collateral * 10_000 < notional_value * maintenance_cr_bps
```

With the default `maintenance_cr_bps = 12000`, that is CR below 120%. Positions are opened at or above `initial_cr_bps` (150% by default), so there is a buffer zone between the tightest permitted open and the liquidation threshold.

<Note>
Liquidation checks use the index TWAP, never the Meteora pool price. Pushing the pool around does not liquidate anyone; only the oracle index, which is itself EMA-smoothed and breaker-guarded, moves CR. See [Circuit Breaker](/concepts/circuit-breaker).
</Note>

## What happens in a liquidation

`liquidate()` is permissionless and cash-only. In v1 it closes the full position in one call; there are no partial liquidations. **No token inventory is needed**: the liquidator does not buy or return any synth, unlike the retired CDP model.

1. The position's loss (its underwater PnL against the index) is **settled to the [insurance fund](/concepts/insurance-fund)** from collateral.
2. The liquidator is paid a bonus from the position's collateral:

```
bonus  = notional_value * liq_bonus_bps / 10_000
payout = min(bonus, collateral remaining after the loss is settled)
```

3. If collateral cannot cover the liquidator's bonus, the insurance fund tops up the difference, up to its available balance.
4. Any collateral left after the settled loss and the bonus is refunded to the position owner.
5. The position is zeroed; rent is refunded on `close_position` if the owner still holds the account.

With the default `liq_bonus_bps = 500`, the liquidator earns a 5% premium over the position's index-valued notional.

## Running the liquidation

Because liquidation is cash-only, the loop is simple: detect, call, get paid.

1. Detect an underwater position (watch `ShortChanged`, `IndexPushed`, and `FundingAccrued` events, or poll positions).
2. Call `liquidate()`. You need no synth and no upfront capital beyond transaction fees.
3. Profit = the bonus paid in SOL, minus transaction fees.

Because there is no synth to acquire, there is no AMM slippage cost and no direction risk in sourcing tokens: the profit is the SOL bonus. See [Running a Liquidator](/guides/running-a-liquidator) for a practical setup.

## Edge cases

- **Frozen market.** Liquidations are blocked while a market is frozen. A bad oracle print should never liquidate anyone, so the breaker pauses liquidations along with everything else.
- **Stale index.** Liquidations also require a fresh index: past `max_index_age_secs` without a push, `liquidate` returns `IndexStale`. Nobody should be liquidated against a price the oracle stopped standing behind. See the [staleness guard](/concepts/price-index#staleness-guard).
- **Insurance exhausted.** If collateral cannot cover the bonus and the insurance fund is empty, the liquidator receives whatever is available. Deeply underwater positions may briefly be unprofitable to liquidate; the bonus and the buffer between 150% and 120% exist to make that rare. Seed the fund with `fund_insurance` to keep the incentive intact.
- **Funding drift.** Funding is folded into the position's settleable PnL, so a position can drift into liquidation on funding alone. Always compute CR from on-chain state, not from your records.

<Warning>
For position owners: withdrawals are held to the initial CR precisely so you cannot exit into the liquidation zone yourself, but a rising index or negative funding drift can still take you there. Watch your CR and top up with `add_collateral`, which works even while a market is frozen.
</Warning>
