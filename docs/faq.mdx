<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="What is my token actually tied to?">
A real collectible's price. Every market is launched against one underlying:
a specific graded card (for example, a [PSA](https://www.psacard.com) 10 Umbreon VMAX) or an NFT
collection's floor (for example, Mad Lads). One million tokens represent one
unit of that collectible, and the token's pool price is pulled toward the
collectible's live price by [funding](/concepts/funding) and cash-settled
shorts. SOL is just what you pay with; it is never the thing being tracked. If
the card doubles, your exposure doubles; if it drops, so does your token.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Is the token backed by real cards or NFTs?">
Not in the vault sense. The token is a synthetic: its price is tethered to the
oracle index by [funding](/concepts/funding), and the short side is
[cash-settled](/concepts/shorts) in SOL against a per-market
[insurance fund](/concepts/insurance-fund). The token's solvency never depends
on custodied items, so there is no lemons problem and no oracle-priced
redemption pot to drain. Markets can additionally hold an
[item reserve and escrow](/concepts/synth-token#item-swaps): a holder of the
registered collectible can swap the real item for tokens (and back) at the
oracle value for a 0.7% fee. That is optional redemption inventory, not what
the supply is backed by.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Is the supply fixed? Can the team mint more?">
No one can mint more, ever. Every ***commas*** token is a fixed-supply SPL
mint created by the [Meteora](https://www.meteora.ag) DBC launch at
**1,000,000,000 tokens**, with the mint authority revoked at creation, which
is what wallets and screeners show as "mint revoked". The whole supply is the
tradeable float; there is no elastic, program-minted supply and no burn.
Shorts are cash-settled, so they never draw or return tokens, and supply stays
exactly 1B for the life of the market.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Why doesn't the token price exactly equal the index?">
The peg is soft. Funding and cash-settled shorts pull the price toward the
index; they do not pin it there. A basis of a few percent is normal and can
persist. If you need exact index exposure at every block, a funding-pegged
synthetic is the wrong instrument.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What happens if the oracle is manipulated?">
The damage is bounded by design. A bad print can only tilt the funding rate
(clamped by `max_funding_bps_per_day`) and shift open shorts' PnL until the
TWAP recovers, and a print that deviates too far from the TWAP trips the
[circuit breaker](/concepts/circuit-breaker) and freezes the commas machinery
instead of entering the index. There is no redemption-against-pooled-money
mechanism for a manipulated price to drain.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What happens if the price feed (the relayer) goes down?">
The index stops updating and `index_last_ts` on the market account shows its
age. Past `max_index_age_secs`, the on-chain [staleness
guard](/concepts/price-index#staleness-guard) stops trusting the last value:
new shorts, withdrawals, closes, funding, and liquidations are rejected with
`IndexStale` until a fresh push lands. Trading on the Meteora pool keeps
working (commas does not control it), and adding collateral keeps working, so
nobody is trapped: the synthetic layer degrades to a safe mode rather than
freezing anyone out of the pool.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Who is the counterparty to my long?">
On a spot buy, whoever sells on the Meteora pool. The synthetic short side is
separate: it is [cash-settled](/concepts/shorts) in SOL against a per-market
[insurance fund](/concepts/insurance-fund), seeded permissionlessly with
`fund_insurance`. The fund pays short profits and absorbs short losses, and
total short open interest is capped by `max_open_interest`.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I redeem the token for a card or an NFT?">
Yes, when the market's item escrow holds one. [Item
swaps](/concepts/synth-token#item-swaps) work in both directions and are
value-based: swap a registered copy for tokens worth exactly one copy at the
oracle value, or return that much token value and take a copy out of escrow.
Both directions pay the 0.7% fee, there is no fixed tokens-per-item rate and no
redemption queue; withdrawals are first-come while escrowed copies last, and a
market whose escrow is empty is back to pure synthetic exposure until someone
swaps a copy in.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Why is collateral SOL only?">
Simplicity and safety in v1: one collateral asset, no collateral price feed
needed beyond the index itself (which is quoted in lamports), and no
multi-asset liquidation math. Note the consequence: positions carry
underlying-versus-SOL relative exposure.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What does it mean when a market is frozen?">
The commas machinery pauses: shorts, funding, liquidations, item swaps, and
oracle pushes. Adding collateral still works, and trading on the Meteora pool
keeps going (commas does not custody it). Markets freeze when the circuit
breaker trips on an outlier oracle print or when the admin freezes manually.
The admin reviews and unfreezes. Balances and positions are untouched while
frozen.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Is there a commas token?">
Yes: a ***commas*** token will launch on [pump.fun](https://pump.fun). Follow
[@commasdotart on X](https://x.com/commasdotart) for the announcement and the
official contract address; anything not posted there is not ours. The protocol
itself remains fully usable without it, and each market's token is the
only token the program creates.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How are fees taken and who gets them?">
Each trade and each item swap pays the [Meteora](https://www.meteora.ag) pool's
0.7% base fee, split 50/50 between the market's fee receiver and the protocol.
The creator half can route to an [identity escrow](/reference/pdas#identity-escrows)
released after X (OAuth), YouTube, or Elite Fourum proof. The
[insurance fund](/concepts/insurance-fund) is seeded permissionlessly with
`fund_insurance`. There are no separate fees on opening or closing shorts
beyond funding.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What chains does commas run on?">
Solana only. The program is written in Anchor, and the design leans on
Solana-native infrastructure (SPL tokens, Meteora pools, Jupiter routing,
[Pyth](https://pyth.network) for the SOL/USD leg, Solana-native marketplaces).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Is commas live?">
Yes. ***commas*** runs on Solana mainnet. The Meteora DBC venue replaces the
retired internal curve; NFT floors read live from Magic Eden, while graded-card
indexes are operator-attested from Collector Crypt for now (a fully automated
graded feed is on the [roadmap](/roadmap)). Independent audit work is also on
the roadmap.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="How is this different from fractionalized NFTs or a 404 token?">
Fractionalization and 404-style hybrids custody the underlying and peg by
two-way redemption, which makes them trackers with vault problems (adverse
selection, custody risk, reflexive unwinding). ***commas*** references the
underlying's price without holding it, and accepts a softer peg in exchange
for eliminating custody entirely. The optional item swap is a bounded door for
the real item, not custody-by-default. See [How it works](/how-it-works).
</Accordion>
