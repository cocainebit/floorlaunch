---
title: "Listing a Market"
description: "How markets get listed in v1: admin-gated creation on Meteora, depth criteria, and open-interest sizing."
---

In v1, market creation is admin-gated: the launchpad service submits the
listing on the creator's behalf, keyed to the admin in the global config. This
is deliberate. The entire safety model assumes the synthetic short side stays
small relative to the real market it references, and that assumption is
enforced at listing time, off chain, by depth criteria. Permissionless listing
with on-chain depth attestations is a [roadmap](/roadmap) item.

## Depth criteria

A listing has to demonstrate that the reference market is deep enough that
the commas market cannot become its price setter.

**Graded cards** (flagship asset class):

- Graded population large enough that no single holder controls the float.
  Mid-tier cards with hundreds or thousands of graded copies qualify; pop-10
  grails do not.
- Steady sale frequency: enough recent prints per lookback window for the
  price feed's estimator (the relayer, see
  [card pipeline](/oracle/card-pipeline)) to be meaningful.
- Multi-venue presence: prices observable on at least two independent venues
  (eBay/TCG aggregators, tokenized marketplaces like Collector Crypt) so the
  cross-source agreement check has something to compare.

**NFT collections** (secondary asset class):

- Sustained trading volume, not a one-week spike.
- Listing depth near the floor: manipulating the floor must require moving
  many listings, not one.
- Holder count and distribution consistent with a real market.

## Sizing the open-interest cap (`max_open_interest`)

`max_open_interest` caps the total short open interest (notional) a market can
carry, denominated in token base units. Shorts are [cash-settled](/concepts/shorts),
so this bounds notional exposure, not a token reserve. It is the on-chain
expression of the depth criteria: the synthetic short side is never allowed to
grow beyond a fixed multiple of what the reference market actually trades. As a
rule of thumb, size it at a small multiple of the collectible's typical
lookback-window sale volume, and revisit as the reference market grows or
shrinks. Because the short side settles against the
[insurance fund](/concepts/insurance-fund), a market's practical short capacity
is the smaller of this cap and what the fund can back.

## What a listing consists of

<Steps>
  <Step title="Underlying spec">
    A precise definition of the unit: for cards, game + set + card + grade
    (for example [PSA](https://www.psacard.com) 10 of one specific card); for NFT collections, the
    verified collection mint. Baskets define members and fixed weights. This
    becomes the relayer config entry.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Market parameters">
    A full `MarketParams` set: oracle windows and breaker, funding constants,
    collateral ratios, the liquidation bonus, and the open-interest cap. See
    [Parameters](/concepts/parameters) for every field. Passing `null` uses the
    global defaults. The trade fee is the [Meteora](https://www.meteora.ag) pool
    base fee (0.7%), not a commas parameter.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Meteora launch">
    The token launches as a Meteora Dynamic Bonding Curve pool with a fresh SPL
    mint (fixed 1B supply, mint authority revoked), branded as the collectible
    via [Metaplex metadata](/concepts/synth-token#identity). The market attaches
    to that mint with
    [`create_external_market`](/reference/instructions#create_external_market),
    starting directly `Live`. The `collection` pubkey is an opaque identifier:
    for cards it identifies the card+grade spec, for NFTs the collection mint.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fund the facilities">
    Optionally fund the [item reserve](/concepts/synth-token#item-swaps) with an
    SPL transfer (to enable item swaps) and seed the
    [insurance fund](/concepts/insurance-fund) with `fund_insurance` (to give
    the short side a counterparty). Both are permissionless top-ups.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Relayer activation">
    The relayer starts pushing the index and the Meteora mark. Most risk
    instructions refuse to run until an index exists, so the short and item-swap
    facilities come online with the oracle feed.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
The program itself is underlying-agnostic. Everything that distinguishes a
card market from an NFT market lives in the relayer configuration and the
listing policy described here, not in the on-chain code.
</Note>
