A ***commas*** token is not a picture of a collectible. It is a token whose price is pulled toward a real collectible's price by a funding tether, with an optional door for the physical item to come in and out. This page walks the loop and the flows.

## The tether is funding, not redemption

The peg is economic, the same shape that holds a perpetual future to its index. When the token's pool price (the [mark](/concepts/amm)) sits above the collectible's [index](/concepts/price-index), [funding](/concepts/funding) pays the short side, and holders of the underlying open [cash-settled shorts](/concepts/shorts) against the rich mark to lock in their price. When the token sits below the index, funding charges shorts and the long side collects that flow, which is the incentive to bid the mark back up. Neither leg needs permission, a market maker, or the team.

The peg is **soft**: funding drifts the price toward the index within a band set by fees, collateral cost, and the funding clamp. It does not pin the price block by block. That softness is the honest price of having no vault and no redemption pot to drain.

## The optional item door

Every market can also hold an **item reserve** and escrow: a holder of the registered collectible can swap the real item for tokens at the oracle value, and any token holder can swap back for a copy while the reserve holds one. This is **optional redemption** at a **0.7%** fee, not the peg and not custody by default:

- **Token trades above the collectible's value** → swapping a copy in pays more than the copy is worth elsewhere (less the fee). Holders of the real thing feed copies in, take tokens, and their selling presses the token back toward fair value.
- **Token trades below the collectible's value** → swapping a copy out is cheap (less the fee). Buying tokens to redeem presses the token back up.

Be honest about the strength of this effect: it is real but **bounded and optional**. It only fires when the token trades far enough from fair value to clear the 0.7% fee, it depends on someone actually holding the registered collectible and choosing to swap, and it is capped by the item reserve's size. It is a genuine door between the token and the physical market, not a mechanical peg, and it is not what the token's solvency depends on.

## Who earns what

| Flow | Where it goes |
| --- | --- |
| 0.1 SOL launch fee | Protocol treasury |
| 0.7% fee on every trade | Half to the market's fee receiver, half to the protocol (the Meteora pool base fee) |
| 0.7% fee on item swaps | Same 50/50 split |
| Funding payments | Between the short side and the [insurance fund](/concepts/insurance-fund), tethering price to the index |

The fee receiver can be an identity (an X account, a YouTube channel, a forum figure), so a community can launch a market and route half its volume fees to the person who actually built that collectible's community, without asking them first. The fee accrues in a keyless escrow and is released after ownership proof via [X (OAuth)](https://x.com), YouTube, or [Elite Fourum](https://elitefourum.com) verification.

## The hedging leg

Holders of the collectible can **open a cash-settled short** to flatten their exposure without selling the real thing: post SOL collateral, and PnL settles in SOL against the index. Hedgers are natural sellers of exposure when the token runs hot and natural closers when it runs cold, which is the structurally motivated short side alongside the funding tether. The [insurance fund](/concepts/insurance-fund) is the bounded counterparty, seeded permissionlessly with `fund_insurance`. See [Hedging](/guides/hedging).
