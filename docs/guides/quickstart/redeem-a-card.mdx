---
title: "Take out the card"
description: "Swap tokens for a real copy of the collectible."
---

A ***commas*** market is not only SOL liquidity: its escrow can hold real copies of the collectible that holders have swapped in. Any of them can be taken out by anyone willing to pay what a copy is worth.

## How redemption works

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check the pool">
    The trade panel's sell side shows how many copies the escrow currently
    holds. If it holds none, there is nothing to take out yet; copies appear
    whenever someone swaps in with the collectible.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Swap it out">
    You pay tokens **equal in value to one copy at the oracle value**, plus the
    0.7% swap fee, and the copy transfers to your wallet. The tokens you pay go
    back into the market's item reserve, so total supply never changes.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The math, concretely

Say the collectible's live index is 0.44 SOL and the token trades at its fair value. Taking out a copy costs about 0.44 SOL worth of tokens, plus the 0.7% fee. If the token trades **below** fair value, a copy costs more tokens (each token is worth less); if it trades **above**, fewer. You always exchange equal value at execution, net of the fee.

<Note>
  Redemptions are first-come. The escrow is a shared pool of copies, not
  per-depositor lockers: swapping a copy in sells it to the market, and any
  holder can be the one who takes it out.
</Note>

This two-way door, collectible in, tokens out; tokens in, collectible out, is the optional physical leg of the market. It is not the peg (funding and cash-settled shorts are; see [How it works](/how-it-works#the-tether)) and it is bounded by the item reserve, but it does let real copies move in and out at fair value. The full loop is walked through in [Economics](/guides/economics).
