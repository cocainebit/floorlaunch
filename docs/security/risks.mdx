<Warning>
floorlaunch v1 is **unaudited and not deployed to any public network**. Everything on
this page should be read as applying to a pre-release system. Do not size positions
you cannot afford to lose.
</Warning>

## The peg is soft

The synth is tethered to the index by arbitrage incentives and a
[clamped funding rate](/concepts/funding), not by redemption at oracle price.
Funding pulls the market price toward the index; it does not nail it there. Expect a
persistent basis of a few percent, wider in volatile stretches. If you buy the synth
expecting tick-for-tick tracking of the underlying, you have misunderstood the
product; see [How It Works](/how-it-works).

## Oracle risk is bounded, not zero

The index comes from the price feed, an off-chain relayer service signing with a single authority key.
The program bounds what a bad or malicious print can do (EMA smoothing,
[circuit breaker](/concepts/circuit-breaker), funding clamp, no oracle-priced
redemption), but bounded damage is still damage: a skewed index can tilt funding
against you or shift collateral checks. A dead relayer degrades the market to a safe
mode: past `max_index_age_secs` the [staleness
guard](/concepts/price-index#staleness-guard) blocks new shorts, withdrawals against
debt, funding, and liquidations until the feed resumes, though trading and repaying
stay open and a long outage still leaves positions unmanageable in the interim.
Details in [Attack Vectors](/security/attack-vectors) and
[Relayer Operations](/oracle/operations).

## Shorts can be liquidated

Opening a short is levered exposure to the underlying rising. If the index moves up
enough that your collateral ratio falls below `maintenance_cr_bps`, anyone may
[liquidate](/concepts/liquidations) you: your debt is cleared against your collateral,
a bonus goes to the liquidator, and in a deep move you can lose your entire
collateral. Underlying markets (cards especially) can gap on news with no intraday
price path to react to.

## The insurance fund is finite

Liquidation shortfalls are covered by a per-market
[insurance fund](/concepts/insurance-fund) seeded at graduation. It can be exhausted.
Past that point, shortfalls are unabsorbed and the market's solvency depends on
remaining overcollateralization.

## Admin powers (v1 centralization)

In v1 a single admin key can: list markets, freeze and unfreeze any market, update
parameters, rotate the oracle authority, and withdraw accumulated protocol fees.
The oracle authority is a second trusted key. There is no governance, no timelock,
and no multisig requirement enforced by the program. Until that changes (see
[Roadmap](/roadmap)), users are trusting the operator not to misuse these powers,
for example by freezing a market at a hostile moment or pushing parameters that
disadvantage open positions.

## Smart contract risk

The program handles pooled lamports and a preminted token reserve with nontrivial
fixed point math (funding indexes, EMA, constant product curves). It has a passing
test suite and no audit. Bugs in money-handling code are the historical cause of most
protocol losses in this category.

## Regulatory posture

A token that provides synthetic exposure to an external asset's price, with a
collateralized short side and liquidations, resembles a derivatives venue in most
jurisdictions. Nothing here is legal advice; users and operators should assess their
own exposure.

## Thin underlyings

The whole design assumes the reference market is deep relative to the synthetic
market. Listing policy and [hedge-reserve sizing](/concepts/parameters) enforce that
assumption, but an underlying can thin out after listing (a card falls out of favor,
a collection dies). A stale, thin reference makes every number downstream of the
index less meaningful.
