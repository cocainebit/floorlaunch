Every ***commas*** market moves through the same lifecycle. This page walks it end to end; each stage links to a concept page with the exact math and parameters.

## What you are actually trading

Every ***commas*** token is priced against a collectible, not against SOL or a dollar peg. One million tokens represent one unit of that collectible: one [PSA](https://www.psacard.com) 10 card, or one floor NFT of a collection. A worked example:

- A market is listed for PSA 10 Umbreon VMAX Alt Art. The card trades around \$2,100 in the real world, so one full unit of tokens is worth about \$2,100 of exposure, and each individual token about \$0.0021.
- The card's real price doubles over the next year. The token's index doubles with it, and funding drags the token's pool price along. Your exposure doubled without you ever buying, grading, storing, or insuring a card.
- The card slumps 30 percent instead. Your token exposure slumps with it. This is price exposure in both directions, not a bet that only pays up.

SOL is the currency you trade with and the collateral hedgers post. It is never the thing the token tracks. Keep that frame through the lifecycle below: every stage exists to keep the token honest about one collectible's price.

## 1. Listing

A market is created for one collectible underlying: a specific card+grade (for example, PSA 10 of a given card), a basket of cards, or an NFT collection's floor. Every launch gets its **own market and its own token**: the token is a fresh SPL mint, so the same collectible can be launched against any number of times, each launch with its own pool and creator. Program-level market creation is admin-gated in v1 and submitted by the launchpad service on the creator's behalf, with listing criteria (sale frequency, depth, population) enforced at listing time. See [Listing a market](/guides/listing-a-market).

## 2. The index goes live

The market's price feed goes live: a signed relayer service reads the collectible's real price (live [Magic Eden](https://magiceden.io) floor for NFT collections; operator-attested [Collector Crypt](https://collectorcrypt.com) floors for graded cards, converted to SOL through [Pyth](https://pyth.network)) and writes it on chain as index observations, the price of one unit of the collectible in lamports. On chain, pushes are rate-limited, smoothed into a time-weighted EMA (the index TWAP), and checked against a [circuit breaker](/concepts/circuit-breaker) that freezes the market's risk machinery if an observation deviates too far from the TWAP. How the price is estimated off chain is covered in [Oracle & Relayer](/oracle/architecture).

<Note>
The on-chain half (push, TWAP, breaker, [staleness guard](/concepts/price-index#staleness-guard)) and the off-chain price feed (the [relayer](/oracle/architecture)) are both live. NFT floors read live from Magic Eden; a fully automated graded-card feed is still pending, so graded prices are operator-attested from Collector Crypt for now.
</Note>

The token launches as a **[Meteora](https://www.meteora.ag) Dynamic Bonding Curve (DBC)** pool with a fresh SPL mint: **fixed 1,000,000,000 supply, mint authority revoked**, the whole supply the tradeable float. The mint's on-chain [Metaplex metadata](/concepts/synth-token#identity) brands it as the collectible so Solscan and terminals render the token *as* the underlying. Buys and sells route through **Jupiter** into the Meteora pool, which charges a **0.7%** base fee. commas attaches an on-chain oracle market to the pool ([`create_external_market`](/reference/instructions#create_external_market)) and the relayer pushes the **index** (the collectible's price) and the **mark** (the live pool price). See [Launch venue](/concepts/launch-curve).

<Note>
There is no internal commas bonding curve any more. The retired v1 venue ran a self-contained curve and AMM inside the program; the current path lists directly on Meteora, so liquidity lives in audited, Jupiter-routed infrastructure. See [Venues](/concepts/amm#venues).
</Note>

## 4. Migration to DAMM v2

As the DBC fills, Meteora **migrates the pool to DAMM v2** on its own schedule. This is Meteora's migration, not a commas graduation gate: commas never holds the curve or the pool, and no commas instruction has to be cranked for the token to keep trading. The oracle market, funding clock, and hedging facility run across both the DBC and DAMM v2 phases without interruption. See [Migration](/concepts/graduation).

## 5. Live phase

Four things run continuously once a market is live.

**Trading.** Longs buy and sell the token on the Meteora pool through Jupiter. The relayer keeps pushing the **mark** (the live pool price) into the same EMA smoothing the funding mechanism reads, so a single-transaction price spike does not move funding or liquidation checks.

**Cash-settled shorts.** Anyone can post SOL collateral and open a short whose PnL settles in SOL against the oracle index (profit when the collectible falls), at a minimum 150% collateral ratio (default). No tokens are drawn and no reserve is touched: the [insurance fund](/concepts/insurance-fund) is the bounded counterparty, and total short open interest is capped by `max_open_interest`. A short closes with `close_position`, which settles PnL directly. See [Shorts](/concepts/shorts).

**Funding.** A permissionless crank compares the mark EMA to the index TWAP and accrues a clamped funding rate to open shorts through a global funding index. See [Funding](/concepts/funding).

**Liquidations.** If a short's collateral ratio falls below maintenance (120% default), any liquidator can close it; the loss is settled against collateral (topped up by the insurance fund if needed), the liquidator earns a bonus, and any remainder returns to the owner. It is cash-only, no tokens move. See [Liquidations](/concepts/liquidations).

**Item swaps.** Holders of the registered collectible (a vaulted-card NFT, a collection NFT) can swap the real item for tokens at the oracle value, and any token holder can swap back for a copy while the item reserve holds one. The swap charges the **0.7%** fee and is sourced from a funded item reserve. It is optional redemption, not custody by default. See [Item swaps](/concepts/synth-token#item-swaps).

## The tether

The token has no fixed-rate redemption pot: [item swaps](/concepts/synth-token#item-swaps) are optional value exchanges, not a peg mechanism, and they depend on item-reserve inventory. So what keeps the pool price near the index?

**Above the index:** shorting is profitable and funding turns positive for shorts. A hedger posts collateral and opens a cash-settled short at the rich mark, expecting the token to fall back toward the index; funding also pays whoever holds the short side while the mark sits above the index. Together these cap sustained upside deviation.

**Below the index:** existing shorts close for a profit (they entered rich, the mark is now cheap) and funding charges shorts while the mark sits under the index, pushing them to close and pushing new shorts to wait. Closing a short does not itself buy the token, but the long side is being paid the funding that shorts pay, which is the incentive to bid the mark back up.

The result is a soft peg: the pool price oscillates around the index within a band set by fees, collateral cost, and the funding clamp, rather than being nailed to it by redemption. That softness is the price of having no vault.

## Why holders are the natural shorts

A synthetic market needs someone willing to be short the underlying. For collectibles, that someone already exists: the holder. A collector sitting on an appreciated card or NFT can hedge by opening a cash-settled short, locking in the current price in SOL terms without selling the physical item, losing community status, or triggering a sale. Their downside on the short is offset by the asset they hold. This gives every market an organic, structurally motivated short side rather than relying on speculators alone.
